## [LLMs Corrupt Documents Through 'Semantic Ablation' in Delegation Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15597) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A new research paper from arXiv (2604.15597) demonstrates that large language models (LLMs) corrupt documents when used for delegation tasks, introducing the concept of 'semantic ablation' where unique, high-entropy content is replaced with generic, probable tokens. This finding is significant because it quantifies a known but poorly understood limitation of LLMs, with implications for agentic systems that rely on LLMs for document processing and decision-making. It highlights the risk of cumulative degradation when LLMs are used repeatedly on the same content. The researchers implemented a basic agentic harness with file reading, writing, and code execution tools, but found that tool use did not prevent corruption. Their methodology uses semantic equivalence across 52 domains, showing that generic similarity measures like LLM-as-a-judge (e.g., GPT-5.4) fail to capture nuanced semantic differences.

hackernews · rbanffy · May 9, 08:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48073246)

**Background**: Large language models generate text by predicting the most probable next token based on training data. 'Semantic ablation' describes how LLMs erode unique, high-information content into generic, safe outputs, similar to how repeated JPEG compression degrades image quality. Agentic AI systems use LLMs as a 'brain' to perform actions through tools, but this research shows that even with tool access, delegation leads to document corruption.

## [WebRTC's low-latency design harms LLM audio prompt accuracy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/9/luke-curley/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Luke Curley, in a blog post responding to OpenAI, highlights that WebRTC's hard-coded low-latency design aggressively drops audio packets, degrading audio quality and making it unsuitable for accurate LLM prompt transmission. This matters because as voice AI and LLM interactions grow, relying on WebRTC for audio input could produce inaccurate responses due to dropped packets. It raises a need for alternative transport protocols that prioritize integrity over low latency. WebRTC is designed for real-time communication like video calls, where low latency is prioritized over quality. Curley notes that even retransmission of audio packets is impossible in browsers, as the implementation is hard-coded for real-time delivery.

rss · Simon Willison · May 9, 01:03

**Background**: WebRTC (Web Real-Time Communication) is a standard for peer-to-peer real-time audio/video communication in browsers. It uses UDP and includes mechanisms like packet dropping and jitter buffers to maintain low latency, which works well for conversational voice but degrades audio quality when packets are lost. For LLM audio prompts, every word matters, so packet loss can significantly alter the prompt.
